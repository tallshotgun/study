Field of Artificial Intelligence or AI, is concerned with not just understanding but also building intelligent entities; machines that can computer how to act effectively and safely in a wide variety of novel situations.

General subfields of AI : learning, reasoning, perception ... AND Specific subfields : chess, maths, architecture, poetry ...

Researchers pursue several different versions of AI.
   Human v/s Rational	
	Some defined intelligence in terms of fidelity to HUMAN PERFORMANCE - does the system mimic how humans operate
	Others prefer an abstract, formal definition : RATIONALITY - does the system achieve optimal, logically correct outcomes

   Thought v/s Behaviour
	Some consider intelligeence to be a property of INTERNAL THOUGHT PROCESS and REASONING
	Others focus on intelligent BEHAVIOUR; an external characterization

Pursuit of human-like intelligence must be somewhat related to psychology, involving observations and hypotheses about actual human behaviour and thought process 
A rationalist approach involvees a combination of mathematics and engineering, and connects to statistics, control theory and economics.

ACTING HUMANLY : The turing test approach

Turing test proposed by Alan Turing was designeed as a thought experiment that would sidestep the philosophical vagueness of the question "Can a machine think?". A computer passed the test if a human interrogator, after posing some 
written question, cannot tell whether the responses come from a person or a computer.

The computer would need the following capabilities : 
	natural language processing 		to communicate succesgully in a human language
	knowledge representation		to store what it knows or hears
	automated reasoning			to answer question and to draw new conclusion
	machine learning			to adapt to new circumstances and to detect and extrapolate patterns

Other researchers proposed a Total Turing Test, which requires interaction with objects and people in the real world
	computer vision	and speech recognition	to percieve the world
	robotics				to manipulate objects and move about


THINKING HUMANLY : The cognitive modeling approach

To say that a program thinks like a human, we must know how humans think :
	introspection			trying to catch our own thoughts as they go by
	psychological experiments	observing a person in action
	brain imaging			observing the brain in action

Once we have a sufficiently precise theory of mind, it becomes possible to express the theory as a computer program. If program's I/O matches corresponding human behaviour, that is evidence that some of the programs's mechanisms
could also be operating in humans.

Not content merely to have their program solve problemsn correctly, but more concrene with comparing the sequence and timing of its reasoning steps to thos of human subject solving the same problems.


THINKING RATIONALLY : The laws of thought approach

Aristotle's syllogisms provided patterns for argument structures that always yielded correct conclusions when given correct premises. These laws of thought were supposed to govern the operation of the mid; their study initiated the 
field called LOGIC.

Logicians developed a precise notation for statements about bjects in the world and the relations amongst them. The logicist tradition in AI hopes to build on such programs to create intelligent systems.

Logic as conventionally understood requires knowledge of the world that is CERTAIN; a condition that in reality is seldom achieved. The THEORY OF PROBABILITY fills this gap, allowing rigorous reasoning with uncertian information.
	It allows the construction of a comprehensive model of rational thought, leading from raw perceptual information to an understanding of how the world works to predictions of the future.

What is does not do is generate INTELLIGENT BEHAVIOUR; rational action.


ACTING RATIONALLY : The rational agent approach

An AGENT is just something that acts. 

All computer programs do something but agents are expected to more : operate autonomously, percieve their environment, persist over a prolonged time period, adapt to change and create and pursue goals,

A RATIONAL AGENT us one that acts so as to achieve the best outcome or, the best expected outcome (when there is uncertainity).

Sometimes part of being a rational agent is making correct inferences, because one way to act rationally is to deduce that a given action is best and then to act on that conclusion.
On the other hand there are ways of acting rationally that cannot be said to involve inference. A REFLEX ACTION.


All the skills needed for the turing test also allow an agent to act rationally. Knowledge representaion and reasoning enable agents to reach good decisions. We need to be able to generate comprehensible sentences in natural language to 
get by in a complex society. We need learning not only for erudition, but also it improves our ability to generate effective behaviour, especially in circumstances that are new.

Rational Agent approach has two advantages :
	1. More general than "laws of thought" approach - Correct inference is just one mechanism for achieving rationality.
	2. More amenable to scientific development.

The standard of rationality is mathematically well defined and completely general.

Over the years, AI has focused on the study and construction of agents that do the right things. What counts as right thing is defined by the objective we provide to the agent. We might call it the STANDARD MODEL.

One important refinement in the standard model is to take account for the fact that perfect rationality, always taking the exactly optimal action, is not feasible in complex environments.
	The computational demands are just too high. 

BENEFICIAL machines
The standard model has been a useful guide for AI research since its inception but it is probably not the right model in the long run as it assumes that we will supply a fully specified objective to the machine.

In the real world it becomes more and more difficult to sprecify the objective completely and correctly.

Value alignment problem : The problem of achieving agreement between our true preferences and the objective we put into the machine.
	The values or objectives we put into the machine must be aligned with those of a human. In a lab or simulator we can just reset and fix the objective but as the field progresses towards increasingly capable intelligent	
		systems that are deployes in the real world this approach is no longer viable. An incorrect objective will produce negative consequences and the more intelligent the system, more negative the consequences.

	It is impossible to anticipate all the ways in which a machine pursuing a fixed objective might misbehave. We dont want machines that are intelligent in the sense of pursuing their objectives; we want them to pursue	
		our objectives. If we cannot transfer those objectives perfectly to the machine we then need a new formulation, one in which the machine is pursuing our objectives but is necessarily uncertaion as to what they are.

	When a machine knows that it doesnt know the complete objective, it has an incentive to act cautiously, ask permission, learn more about our preferences through observation and to defer to human control.
	Ultimately we want agents that are PROVABLY BENEFICIAL TO HUMANS.

	
INTELLIGENT AGENTS

   An agent is anything that can be viewed as percieving its environment through sensons and acting upon that environment through actuators.

   The environment can be anything and everything. In practice it is just the part of that everything whose state we care about when designing the agent, the part that affects what the agent percieves and is affected by the agent's actions.

	Percept : The content an agent's sensors are percieving. 
	Percept sequence : Complete history of everything the agent has ever perceived.

   An agent's choice of action at any given instant can depend on its builtinknowledge and on the entire percept sequence observed to date, but not on anything it has not percieved.

	Agent function : Maps any given percept sequence to an action and describes the agent's behaviour.

	   Externally we can create a table by trying out all possible percept sequence and recording which actions the agent does in response. This is an external characterization.
	   Internally the agent function for an artificial agent will be implemented by an agent program.
	
	Agent function is an abstract mathematical description, while agent program is a concrete implementation, running within some physical system.

   GOOD BEHAVIOUR : Concept of rationality
	
	A rational agent is one that does the right thing. But what does it mean to do the right thing?

	Consequentialism : An agent's behaviour is evaluated by its consequences.

	   When an agent is put in an environment, it generates a sequence of actions according to the perecepts it receives. This sequence of actions causes the environment to go through a sequence of stated.
	 	If this sequence is desirable, then the agent has performed well. The notion of desirability is captured by a performance measure that evaluates any given sequence of environment states.
	
	   Humans have their own desire so rationality for humans has to do with their success in choosing actions that produce sequence of env states that are desirable from their POV.
	   Machines do not have desires of their own; the performance measure initially at least is in the mind of the designer of the machine or the users it is designed for. Some agent designs have explicit representation of performance measure while other designs it is implicit, the agent may do the right thing without knowing why.


	It is better to design performance measures according to what one actually wants to be achieved in the environment, rather than according to how one thinks the agent should behave.

   RATIONALITY
	What is rational at any given time depends on four things:
	   
	   The performance measure that defines the criterion of success
	   The agent's prior knowledge of the environment
	   The actions that the agent can perform
	   The agent's percept sequence to date

	Definition of rational agent

	   For each possible percept sequence, a rational agent should select an action that is expected to maximize its performance measure, given the evidence provided by the percept sequence and whatever built-in-knowledge the agent has.
 
   OMNISCIENCE, LEARNING and AUTONOMY
	
	There is a distinction between rationality and omniscience.
	   An omniscient agents knows the actual outcome to its actions and can act accordingly; but omniscience is impossible in reality.

	RATIONALITY is not the same as PERFECTION. Rationality maximized expected performance, while perfection maximizes actual performance.
	   Our definition of rationality does not require omniscience, because then the rational choice depends only on the percept sequence to date.
	   We must also ensure that we haven't inadvertently allowed the agent to engage in decidedly underintelligent activities.

	Information gathering : Doing actions in order to modify future percepts, is an import part of rationality.
	   Our definition also requires a rational agent not only to gather information but also to learn as much as possible from what it percieves.

	A rational agent should be autonomous and rely on its own percepts and learning processes not just on the prior knowledgre of its designer.
	   It should learn what it can to compensate for partial or incorrect prior knowledge.

NATURE OF ENVIRONMENTS

   TASK ENVIRONMENT : The problems to which the rational agents are solutions.
	In designing an agent, the first step must always be to specify the task environment as fully as possiblle.

	For specifying a task environment we must specify PEAS - Performsnce monitor, Environment, Actuators, Sensors
	
	Properties of task environments :
	   
	   Fully observable vs Partially observable
		If agent's sensors give it access to the complete state of environment at each point of time, task env is fully observable.
		A task env is effectively fully observble if the sensors detect all aspects relevant to the choice of action and relevance in turn depends on the performance measure.
		Fully observable environments are convenient because the agent need not maintain any internal state to keep track of the world.

		An environment might be partially observable because of noisy and inaccurate sensors or because parts of the state are simply missing from the sensor data.

		If the agent has no sensors at all, the environment is unobservable.

	   Single agent vs Multi agent 
		If an agent B's behaviour when trying to maximize its performance measure, it minimizes agent A's performance measure, this is a competitive multiagent environment.
		It can also be partially or fully cooperative.

	   Deterministic vs nondeterministic
		If next state of the env is completely determined by current state and actio executed by the agent, then env is said to be deterministic.

	   Stochastic vs nondeterministic
		Stochastic is when the env explicitly deals with probabilities.
		Nondeterministic when possibilities are listed without quantification,

	   Episodic vs Sequential
		
	   Static vs dynamic
		If an env can change while agent is delibrating then it is dynamic.

	   Discrete vs Continuous

	   Known vs unknown
		In a known env the outcomes for outcomes for all actions are given. The env is known to the agent.
		If unknown agent will have to learn.

STRUCTURE OF AGENTS
   The job of I is to design agent program that implements agent function. This program will run on some sort of computing device with physical sensors and actuators, called the agent architecure
	agent = architecture + program

   The program we choose has to be approrpriate for the architecture. 

   SIMPLE REFLEX AGENTS
	Simplest kind of agent. These select ations on the basis of the current percept, ignoring the rest of the percept history. Percept sequence is smaller.
	
	Condition-action rule : Some visual or other input triggers some established connection in the agent to take an action.
	   If A then B.

	A general and flexible approach is first to build a general-purpose interpreter for condition-action rules and then to create rule sets for specific task environments.

	A simple refle agent will only work if the correct decision can be made on the basis of just the current percept; only if the environment is fully observable.

	   In partially observable environments infinite loops are often unavoidable. Escaping from these is possible if the agent can randomize its actions.
	   A randomized simple reflex agent might outperform a deterministic one.

   MODEL BASED REFLEX AGENT
	Most effective way to handle partial observability is for the agent to keep track of the part of the world it can't see now.
	   Agent should maintain some sort of internal state that depends on the percept history and thereby reflects atleast some of the unobserved aspects of the current state.

	Updating this internal state information as time goes by requires two kinds of knowledge to be encoded in the agent program in some form.
	   First information about hwo the world changes over time, which can be divided into two parts
		The effects of the agent's actions and how the world evolve indepedently of the agent.
	   	This is called a transition model where this knowledge about how the world works is implemented.

	   Second information about how the state of the world is reflected in the agent's percepts.
		This kind of knowledge is called a sensor model.

	Together the transition and sensor model allow an agent to keep track of the state of the world to the extent possible given the limits of agent's sensore.
	An agent that uses such models is called a model-based agent.

   GOAL BASED AGENTS
	In order to make the correct decisions, the agents not only needs a current state description but also a goal that describes the situations that are desirable.

   UTILITY BASED AGENTS
	Goals alone are not enough to generate high quality behaviour in most environments.They just provide a crude binary distinction between happy and unhappy states.
	
	A more general performane measure should allow a comparison of different world states acc to exactly how happy they wiuld make the agent.
	Instead of calling it happy we call it the term utility.

	A performance measure assigns a score to any given sequence of environment states, so it can also easily distinguish between more and less desirable ways of action.
	An agent's utility function is an internalization of the performance measure.
	
	Provided that the internal utility function and external performance measure are in agreement, an agent that chooses actions to maximize its utility will be rational according to external performance measure.

   LEARNING AGENTS
	Instead of programming a complte agent program by hand we can build learning machines and then teach them.
	Learning has more advantages like it allows the agent to operate in unknown environments and become more competent than its initial knowledge alone migth allow.

	Four components :
	
	   Learning Element - Responsible for making improvements
	   Performane element - Responsible for selecting external actions.
	   Critic - Gives feedback to the learning element on how the agent is doing and determines how the performance element should be modified to do better in future.
	   Problem generator - Responsible for suggesting actions that will lead to new and informative experiences.
